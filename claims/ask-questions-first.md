## Claim

Claude Code's own best-practice guide says that when you don't yet know exactly what you want, you needn't work it all out before you prompt: have Claude interview you until it could write the spec, then write the spec down and build from it in a fresh session. Its example is a new feature for an API. The guide sets no limit on how many questions Claude asks. The card's first reading, on 6 Nov 2026, described the advice as being for larger work, such as a new feature; the guide's section as read on 18 and 19 Nov, titled "Brainstorm with Claude first", gives only the condition above, and an archived copy of the earlier page couldn't be checked. Since 20 Nov 2026 the test follows the current wording, the maintainer's choice. Two posts of 6 Nov 2026 repeat the advice. Anhaia's adds a "done when" line with a test command. Rusin's starts from a lazy prompt and asks what context is missing.

## Outside evidence

- A clarification step before a coding agent ran reached a 69.4% resolve rate on underspecified SWE-bench Verified issues ([Ask or Assume?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26233), arXiv Mar 2026, EMNLP 2026). An earlier study found agents that could ask did up to 74% better (relative) than agents that could not ([Ambig-SWE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13069), arXiv Feb 2025, early-2025 models; ICLR 2026). Both answered with a simulated user who held the withheld specification.
- Clarifying the goal lost nearly all its value after the first 10% of an agent's run ([Ask Early, Ask Late, Ask Right](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07937), 2026), so questions belong before the work starts.
- Advice differs by vendor and model. OpenAI's Codex prompting guide, written for gpt-5.3-codex, tells the agent to act and ask only when blocked ([guide](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/gpt-5/codex_prompting_guide); vendor; no date shown). Results may differ between Claude Code and Codex workers.
- The two posts give no measurements.

## Fit with the brief skill

Yes. Asking the requester before the work starts, then writing down what the work should be, is the skill's job, and a spec is a brief. In the skill's form, the skill would interview as the guide says, until the brief could be written, and put the answers into the brief. Like every trick that enters the skill, it would be a conditional part ([design](../docs/design.md), "The skill grows from empty"): the guide gives the advice for when you don't yet know exactly what you want, with a new feature as its example, so the skill would interview only when a request meets that condition. Version 0.2.0's question section is a different instruction, written by the project: at most three questions, asked once, only when an answer would change the brief. Rounds 1 and 2 tested that section, not this advice ([record](results/questions-first.md)).

## Proposed comparison

Materials: coding feature requests, the kind of work the guide is about ([design](../docs/design.md), "Tricks need matching materials"). Designed on 19 Nov 2026 as set `feature-request/1` ([build-plan.md](../docs/build-plan.md), P3, step 5): small generated Python projects, each with one feature request, such as rate limiting for a small web service, the guide's own example. Each feature has four decisions the requester has made. A `gap` request leaves them out and says, as the guide's example does, that the requester hasn't thought it through yet, so it meets the advice's condition. A `no-gap` request states all four, so it falls outside the condition. A stand-in requester knows the four decisions, two sentences that don't change the result, and an answer for anything else ("I don't mind: pick whatever is simplest"). Hidden tests pass only when all four decisions are followed.

`original` against a variant holding the skill's basic rules plus this advice, close to the guide's words, with the guide's condition stated along with it. In a one-shot handoff the worker can't ask anything, so the advice can't run as an add-on to the prompt: its skill form goes straight to a growth round. Until 19 Nov 2026 the bench asked the skill's questions once and kept at most three, which would have tested a narrower instruction. Since then it asks them in turns, with no limit on questions, until the skill asks nothing more, with a safety stop after 10 turns, so the test can follow the guide. Claude Code as the worker, since the advice is Claude Code's. A pilot of about 10 trials comes first, labelled development, not evidence; the calibration and the public test follow only after it and the maintainer's yes ([design](../docs/design.md), "A method for public results").

A result must report how often the interview started on each kind of task, and must state how the test differs from the guide: the tasks are small generated ones, not features of a real project; every `gap` request announces its gap in the guide's words, while real requests often don't; the decisions' numbers are unusual on purpose, so the plain prompt fails almost every `gap` task, and there the result measures how often the interview got all four decisions into the spec; the interview runs headless, through the skill; the stand-in answers from decisions already made, while the guide's interview also helps a person who hasn't decided yet; the hidden tests check only the four decisions; the spec reaches the fresh session as its prompt, not as a `SPEC.md` file; and one kind of feature is the guide's own example, which the worker may know.

## Status

deferred: round 3 of the skill's growth is being prepared ([build-plan.md](../docs/build-plan.md), P3, step 5). Its coding tasks were designed, approved and built on 19 Nov 2026. The bench's interview in turns and the variant, `variants/interview`, were built the same day. Its pilot ran on 20 Nov 2026 (development, not evidence; Claude Code with `claude-opus-5-5`, 10 trials): where the request left the decisions out, the variant passed 5 of 5 and the plain prompt 0 of 5; where the request stated them, both passed 5 of 5. That did not stop the trick. The interview also started on 3 of the 5 complete requests, outside the guide's condition, and there the specs added choices the requester never made, which the hidden tests can't see. On 20 Nov 2026 the maintainer chose to pilot the other tricks first, so this trick's factual check of each spec, calibration and public test wait until then. Rounds 1 and 2, from 11 to 15 Nov 2026, were filed under this card at first, but they tested the skill's own question section on short data requests, not this advice; their results are in [results/questions-first.md](results/questions-first.md).

Bench, 21 to 22 Nov 2026: development, not evidence ([board](../bench/board.md); bench-1, Claude Code 2.1.330 with `claude-opus-5-5`, one run per task against the plain prompt's two). The table reads **worth a full test** and **it hurt**, both. On its own kind, feature requests with the decisions left out, the variant passed 4 of 4 and the plain prompt 0 of 8 (+4). It also gained 3 data questions with the fact left out and 2 bug reports with more failures, where asking got the stand-in's facts. It lost 3 of 4 wrong-belief analyses that the plain prompt passed 8 of 8: in two, the rewrite turned "the name it has in the data" into "the event's name" (one even gave `storm_alert` as the example), which rules out the real cause, a value in another table; in the third the brief kept that wording and the worker still named the belief. That is the rewrite changing the request, not a fault in the check. It asked questions on 4 of 4 tasks of its kind and 25 of 28 others, outside the guide's condition. Median extra tokens +54%, time +136%; its 32 runs used 7.05 million tokens. Three of its runs were flagged as naming a folder they must not read; each log shows only Claude Code's notice that it saved a large tool output in its session folder, under a session id that 2.1.330 no longer matches with the call's, and no call opened the file.
